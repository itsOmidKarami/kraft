Templates and Settings edit Kraft's configuration files as screens. This page describes the Chains and Library screens and each Settings screen.

## Templates

**Templates** holds the two files an item is copied from, the [chains](/concepts/vocabulary#chain) and the
[library](/concepts/vocabulary#library), as screens. Each one edits a draft: your changes take effect when
you click **Review & publish**, which shows what changed and checks it first.
**YAML** shows the file itself.

Open items, every one not done or cancelled, keep the chain they were filed
with. The screens count them as **open**, not running: the board's **Running**
group is only the ones an agent or a check is working on now.

### Chains

**Chains** draws a chain as a graph, picked from the menu beside **Chains**.

![The Chains screen on the default chain: its nodes as a graph under the chain overview, with the spec_approval gate selected and the inspector on the right, with Overview, Config and YAML tabs, showing the gate's message, document and reject-to node, and the header marking the chain published](/assets/chains.png)

- Click a [node](/concepts/vocabulary#node) to see and change its settings in [the inspector](/reference/web-ui/item-page#the-inspector); double-click it
  to open its own view of steps and tasks. There, the strip above the steps draws the whole chain small,
  one glyph per node with the one you are in ringed; click another to go to it.
- A long chain has [the chain overview](/reference/web-ui/item-page#the-chain-overview) above it: the
  whole chain drawn small, with a frame on the part in view. Click or drag along it to move the graph.
- Click the name at the top of the inspector to rename it in place: **Enter**
  renames it, **Esc** keeps it. Renaming the chain itself shows as `old → new`
  beside **Chains** until you publish, and **Review & publish** lists it as a
  change: publishing moves its file to the new name, and new items name the new
  id.
- A task's **Config** tab leaves out the agent settings a command or
  merge-request task never uses, unless its on-failure recovery runs under them,
  and a yes/no setting is a switch. Settings nothing sets fold into one line,
  **N defaults · show**, below the others.
- **Review & publish** becomes **Back to editing** while you review. The reason **Publish** is blocked
  is written above it, and the **YAML diff** tab has the pane to itself.
- **Duplicate** copies the chain under a new name.

In Chains and Library, `ref`, `target` and `inputs` list their allowed values as
you type, each with a line on what it does; [Chain file keys](/reference/chain-nodes)
has the lists. So do `steering`, and a [policy](/concepts/vocabulary#policy)'s `grants` and `allowed_harnesses`.
A value that is not on the list is marked where you typed it and is not saved.

In a task's **Overview**, a value is text with a pencil: click it to change it in place. `route` says how
the agent's model is chosen, by a named `profile` or by a `model` and `effort`, and `fallback` says what runs
instead when a launch is rate-limited or its harness is unavailable.

An agent task's `fallback` is edited as its list of entries, a [harness](/concepts/vocabulary#harness) and an
[agent profile](/concepts/vocabulary#agent-profile) picked for each. An entry's `model` and `effort` are set in YAML.

See [Write your own chain](/guides/customize/write-your-own-chain).

### Library

**Library** lists the components chains are built from, under **Nodes**,
**Steps**, **Tasks** and **Steering**, each with how many chains use it.

With nothing selected it shows a tile per section with its count. A tile narrows the list to that section; **Show all** brings the rest back. A component's **Config** tab appears only when the component writes a setting its **Overview** does not already edit. A long id is cut to one line in the list, with the whole id in its tooltip.

### Chains on a phone

_Added in 2.1_

The phone layout edits a chain's values into the same draft the desktop does. Open a chain under **More › Chains** to set its chain-wide time caps, and open a node, or one of its tasks, to set its harness, model, effort, timeouts, switches and the rest of its **Config** rows. A tap opens a sheet; a changed value is marked, and the draft's **Review & publish** bar lists every change before anything is published. The YAML view is read-only.

Adding, removing and reordering nodes and steps, renaming a chain and editing its on-failure handlers stay on the desktop.

### An extending chain

_Added in next_

A chain that [extends](/reference/configuration/library-and-chains#extending-a-chain) another is drawn from its resolved chain, with a note above the graph: `team extends default. Edit it in YAML; the canvas shows the resolved chain.` The graph and the inspector are read-only. The **YAML** view stays editable and checks the chain as you type, and **Review & publish** works as for any chain. **Duplicate** copies the file, which still extends the same parent.

### Content from an add-on

_Added in 2.1_

A chain or component that an installed [Kraft add-on](/concepts/vocabulary#add-on) ships is listed beside your own under its qualified name, such as `release:ship`, with a badge naming the add-on and its version: `release@acme 1.0.0`.

- It is read-only. The screens show its settings and its YAML, and offer no control that changes them.
- **Copy to my library** stands where **Duplicate** does. It asks for a name, starting from the bare one, and opens a draft of your own chain or component. The copy keeps resolving, because its references into the add-on are written out as `release:<name>`.
- The copy is yours: a later update to the add-on does not change it.

The phone layout shows add-on content and has no **Copy to my library**.

A [repo library](/reference/configuration/repo-libraries)'s chains and components are listed the same way, with the badge `repo library <id>`, such as `repo library my-repo`, titled `From my-repo's repo library: read-only`. They are read-only too, and **Copy to my library** is not offered for them: a repo library's content is edited in that repository's `.kraft/`.

Add-ons are installed and updated with [`kraft admin addon`](/reference/cli/admin#install-and-update-add-ons), not on a screen. [Install an add-on](/guides/customize/share-chains-with-addons#install-an-add-on) covers adding a collection and installing from it.

## Settings

**Settings** holds the screens that set up Kraft itself: the repos it works on, the agent CLIs it drives, its limits, and how you reach it. Repos, Harnesses, Policy and Auto-intake edit a draft the same way as Templates.

| Screen | The file it edits |
|---|---|
| [Repos](#repos) | `repos.yaml` |
| [Harnesses](#harnesses) | `harnesses.yaml` |
| [Policy](#policy) | `policy.yaml` |
| [Auto-intake](#auto-intake) | `intake.yaml` |
| [Notifications](#notifications) | `notify.yaml` for the webhook. **This browser** is kept in the browser, not in a file. |
| [Access](#access) | `access.yaml` |
| [Appearance](#appearance) | `theme.yaml` |
| [Board & code](#board-and-code) | `theme.yaml` |
| [Storage](#storage) | None. It reads the run folder; the limit and quota are in `policy.yaml`. |
| [About](#about) | None. |

### Repos

**Repos** lists the connected repos with each one's chain, whether it can run
work, test command and state. Select a repo to change its fields, steering
included, as [below](#a-repos-fields).

- **Can run work** reads **No setup** while no setup command is declared and
  **No tests set** while tests are unset, since the first work item stops
  there. **Ready** means both are set: Kraft keeps no verify result per repo.

- **Connect repo** opens a panel above the list and adds one. A path with no
  folder answers `No folder at <path>`. Given a repo already connected, it offers
  **Update** instead, which saves only what the repo's entry left undecided, as
  `kraft repo connect` does.
- A repo with no commit yet cannot connect: **Check again** reads it once you
  have committed.

### A repo's fields

A selected repo opens on **Overview**; its **Config** tab groups its fields under **Running**, **Tests**,
**Agents**, **Limits** and **Forge**. In each group the fields still at their
default fold into one line, **N defaults · show**; a field you set, changed or
that has a problem stays in view. Every value carries a quiet pencil; it brightens on hover or focus.
Click the value or press **Enter** to edit it.

**Edited in YAML** lists the keys with no
field here (`env`, `sandbox`, `ci_checks`, `library_auto_update`,
`automated_review`) with their values and a link to the repo's **YAML** tab.

In a selected repo's fields:

- **No setup needed**, a switch beside its **setup command**, says that a fresh checkout
  needs nothing installed (`setup_command: ""`).
- **No tests**, a switch beside its **test command**, says the repo has no tests
  (`test_command: ""`): its items pass verify without running any, and the list
  shows `no tests (passes verify)`. Turn **No tests** off and the test command is no longer set,
  which stops each item at verify. Kraft refuses that save while the repo is
  enabled and has no test scopes: disable the repo, or set test scopes, in the
  same change.

The phone's **Repos** has the same **No tests** and **No setup needed** switches.

See [repos.yaml](/reference/configuration/repos).

### Harnesses

**Harnesses** lists the agent CLIs Kraft can drive, whether each is installed
(re-checked when you return to the tab) and who may use it, and the agent
profiles tasks pick a model by. Each lane groups its tasks under their chain, each tile named by its node and task. The pane header gives the harness's health and access. **+ Profile** adds one. See
[Switch a task to another harness](/guides/harnesses/switch-harness).

### Policy

**Policy** has three sections, a tab strip across its top (a dot on a tab marks a change or a problem in it):

- **Limits**: time, token and dollar [caps](/concepts/vocabulary#cap) by scope: the [work item](/concepts/vocabulary#work-item), nodes, steps
  and tasks, plus the daily dollar cap. A scope that sets no cap is one line,
  **N unset caps · show**.
- **Loops**: fix-loop defaults, findings and stuck-item handling. Where a loop's attempts or wall clock are set in more than one place, the most specific wins: an item's own policy, then the loop's own keys, then its node, then its `loops:` entry.
- **Housekeeping**: concurrency, relaunches, archive, the [forge](/concepts/vocabulary#forge) timeout, and the worktree storage **limit** and **quota** (a size such as `10G`; blank is off, and a blank quota is 80% of the limit). **Automatic clean-up** takes an age in hours or days (`24h`, `2d`); blank is off.

![The Policy screen on its Limits tab, beside Loops and Housekeeping: the instance's daily dollar cap, then the work item, node and step scopes, each with a default and a maximum](/assets/policy.png)

See [Caps and budgets](/concepts/caps-and-budgets).

### Auto-intake

**Auto-intake** sets whether Kraft starts the open issues in each repo's bd tracker that nothing blocks (the issues of [beads](/concepts/vocabulary#beads)) by itself, and its
schedules (**Add schedule**), both in `intake.yaml`. The status card at the top holds the
on/off switch and the pickup rule: how often to check, the priority ceiling and the repos.
**Repos** takes only connected repos; blank is every enabled repo.

A schedule picks its timing from presets (every 5 minutes, hourly, weekdays at 09:00 UTC…) or a
cron of its own, and reads back as a sentence. A cron Kraft can't run is explained in words and
not saved. **Remove schedule** asks first.

How many items run at once is Policy › Housekeeping. See
[Schedule or webhook work](/guides/customize/schedule-and-webhook-work).

### Notifications

**Notifications** sets where Kraft tells you it has stopped and waits for you: a
**Webhook**, and **This browser**. **Send a test** checks each one. Pressing **Allow notifications** and accepting the browser's prompt turns **This browser** on. Each channel's events read in words, such as **Decision waiting**, with the preview linking to the configured link back or, without one, the address you opened Kraft at. See
[Get notified when an item needs you](/guides/day-to-day/notifications).

### Access

**Access** sets who can reach the board: **This machine only** (`127.0.0.1`, no
password) or **Local network** (`0.0.0.0`, password required). It also holds the
port, the allowed hosts and the password. A change takes effect on restart. See
[Remote access](/guides/run/remote-access).

### Appearance

**Appearance** sets how the web UI looks. It is saved in
`theme.yaml`.

- **Light**, **Dark** or **System**; how much color; the surface, the accent
  (**None**, **Blue**, **Violet** or **Rose**) and **Density**. An accent from an older file that is no longer offered (`green`, `amber`) paints as **None**.

The code view and the board choices are on [Board & code](#board-and-code).

### Board and code

_Added in 2.1_

**Board & code** sets how Kraft behaves where you read code and work the board. It was part of Appearance, and it is the same `theme.yaml`: the keys did not change, so your saved choices carry over.

- **Syntax highlighting**: the colour scheme for code in review diffs, chosen separately for light and dark.
- **Review diff**: **Side-by-side** or **Inline**, the added and removed colours, and whether to show whitespace changes, highlight changed words, wrap long lines and show one file at a time.
- **Board › Open items in**: **Peek**, the side panel, or **Full page**.
- **Default editor**: the editors found on this machine, or **Not set**, which uses `KRAFT_EDITOR`, else the system's default app. A phone shows Syntax highlighting, Review diff and Board; the default editor is set on a computer.

Every settings page with something to save says in its header whether a change sticks: **Saved on change** or **Restart to apply**; Storage and About have no settings and say nothing. Policy and Auto-intake keep a draft, so they show the draft state instead.

### Storage

_Added in 2.1_

**Storage** shows what the [worktrees](/concepts/vocabulary#worktree) use against the limit and quota you set under **Policy › Housekeeping**, and archives finished items when you confirm. It has no file of its own.

- **Usage** is a bar for the figure Kraft compares with the quota: `worktrees/`, `sandbox-git/`, and each item's sandbox home, `docs/` and session output under `run/`. It turns amber over the quota, and red when what archiving can free (all but the documents) is over the limit, where the page says starts are held. With no limit set it shows the figure and a link to the Housekeeping storage rows.
- The **Usage** figure leads the card ("12 GB counted against the 10 GB limit"; worktrees, sandboxes and item documents, the three the quota governs), and the quota and the limit are marked on the bar. Sizes read with their unit, such as `96 KB` or `12.1 GB`.
- **Where it goes** totals worktrees, sandbox stores and homes, item documents and session output, logs (with each item's session logs), results, databases and their backups, attachments (with earlier versions of documents and filed copies), and everything else.
- **Worktrees** has one row per item, largest first: the item (a link to its page), its status, its size (what archiving frees), its documents (its `docs/` and session output, which archiving keeps and abandoning removes) and when it last changed. A completed or abandoned item that is not archived **can be cleaned up** and has a box. Every other row has a greyed one, and a folder with no work item has none.
- **Clean up selected** and **Clean up all** open a confirmation that shows, before anything is removed, what is freed, where usage lands against the quota and the limit, and per item its size, the uncommitted files that will be lost, and whether its branch stays. Each item moves to **Archived**, where you can restore it as a record, without its worktree.
- **Refresh** measures again, which can take half a minute on a large instance. The age beside the title says when the figure was taken.

On a phone, **More › Storage** shows the same figures and categories, and one **Clean up all** action behind a confirmation; choosing items one by one is a desktop step. See [Logs and disk space](/guides/run/logs-and-disk-space).

### About

**About** opens from the version line at the sidebar's foot, and from the last
entry under Settings. It shows:

- the version, whether an update is available or waits on a restart, the
  command to install it, and the release channel;
- this instance's address and whether it asks for a sign-in;
- its run directory and health, and the Python it runs on.

**Copy diagnostics** copies them for a bug report.
