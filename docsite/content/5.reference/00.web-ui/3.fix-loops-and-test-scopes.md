---
title: Fix loops and test scopes
description: How a node's own view shows a fix loop's rounds, repair and judge, and a changed-test-scope task's frame, chips and panels.
---

A node's own view, opened from [An item's page](/reference/web-ui/item-page#the-chain-graph), draws the tasks inside the node. This page names the two specialist drawings there: a node with a fix loop and a changed-test-scope task.

## A node with a fix loop

A node's own view draws the loop as a dashed arc under the steps, and once the loop has
run, its **repair** tasks and the **judge** sit on it as tasks of their own: click one
to open it. A loop with several repair steps, such as a repair and then a sync,
shows each in order, left to right, with the judge last.

### A step that runs its tasks in parallel

A loop step with more than one task runs them together, so it is one box on the
loop, captioned **n in parallel** and in the worst state among its tasks. Click
it, or press **Enter** on it, to select it. Its panel lists its tasks as the
round shown ran them, and each row opens that task.

The step opens in place while it, or one of its tasks, is selected: a frame
holds the step's tasks, and the loop forks to each of them and joins again. The
frame's head is the step itself. **Esc** from inside the frame, or its **✕**,
folds it back to its box, and so does picking anything else. A link to the step
or to one of its tasks opens it.

A loop with such a step is drawn as a level lane and not an arc: down from the
node's end, along the bottom, and up into its start, with every stop on one
line. A loop whose steps each have one task keeps the arc.

### Picking a round

Beside the zoom, **round n of m** says which round the canvas shows. It starts on
the newest. Open it to pick an earlier round, newest first, with what became of
each; the canvas then shows every task as that round ran it, the repair that went
on from it and the [judge](/concepts/vocabulary#judge) that came after it.
**latest ↩** goes back. The pick is yours until you leave the node.

### Picking a pass

The chain can run a node again: a gate rejection sends the item back, a retry
reruns the node, or its base branch moves. Each time is a
[pass](/concepts/vocabulary#pass). A pass counts its rounds from 1 and keeps
its own tasks, logs and scopes.

On a node the chain ran more than once, **pass n of m** sits beside the round.
It starts on the newest. Open it to pick an earlier pass, newest first, with
what started each: **after a reject at local_review**, **after a retry** or
**after a base change**. The canvas and every panel then show that pass, and
the round menu lists its rounds. **latest ↩** goes back. A node that ran once
shows no pass.

A gate's reviewer and a node's escalation are in no pass, so their runs stay
together. A pass that an agent's retry started keeps the count of the one
before it, so its rounds start where that one stopped.

### A task in a round

A repair or judge that has not run in a round is drawn dashed and faded: the
judge never runs before the first repair, so round 1's reads
**skipped · first repair**.

A task's panel in such a node names the round in its subtitle (**agent task ·
round 2 of 3 · running 41s**) and shows that round's run, so the
**attempt n of m ▾** menu appears only when the round itself ran the task more
than once.

## A changed-test-scope task

A changed-test-scope task (`kraft.verify_changed_test_scopes`) opens in place
while it, or one of its scopes, is selected: its box becomes a frame with one row
per repository, in the order the task visits them (a [workspace](/concepts/vocabulary#workspace)'s root first, then
its members), and each repository's scopes as chips. A reduced-motion setting skips the move
and crossfades instead.

### The frame

- Scopes that run one after another are joined in a line by arrows, and a task
  set to `execution: parallel` stacks them in a fork.
- An item in one repository has one row, drawn as its chips alone: no number,
  no repository name and no status under it, and a heading that says nothing
  of repos. While it has no chips, the row says **waiting** or **not reached**.
- The frame is as wide as its heading or its longest row, whichever is wider,
  and the steps after it move over to make room, so no row scrolls where the
  scroll wheel pans the canvas.

### What a chip says

A chip says how that scope went and how long it took, and reads as the scope's
command (an area's setup reads **setup · area**); hover it for the whole command
and the paths it covers.

| You see | It means |
|---|---|
| a dashed **waiting** chip | a scope the round picked and has not started |
| **not reached**, with no chips | a repository after the first one that failed |
| **new** | against the round before it, a scope picked for the first time |
| a dashed **not picked** chip | a scope that ran last round and is not picked now; it stays in its row. A repository that ran nothing this round shows no chips at all |

### A chip's panel

A chip opens the scope's own panel:

- its command and the paths it covers;
- its repository;
- how it went in every other round (**passed**, **failed**, **running**,
  **waiting**, **not picked**, or **repo not reached**);
- its log.

The open task's own **Overview** says how many of the round's scopes passed, how
many repositories were reached and how its scopes run, with a key to **new**,
**not picked** and the dashed ring.

### Stepping back and closing

**Esc** steps back one: from a scope to its task, from the open task back to its
box, then the panel folds as usual. Picking anything else, clicking the canvas, or
the frame's **close** also folds it.
