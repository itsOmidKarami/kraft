A stopped [work item](/concepts/vocabulary#work-item) appears under **Needs
you** (`needs_human` in `kraft view list`); start here too when one waits and
nothing moves. Find the text Kraft shows in the tables under
[Find your reason](#find-your-reason): each row has the fix, or links to an
entry that has it. A pending gate and the two waits on CI or a review are not
stops:
[Escalation turns and things that are not a stop](#escalation-turns-and-things-that-are-not-a-stop)
and [Waiting on CI or a review](#waiting-on-ci-or-a-review) cover them.

![The board's Needs you group with seven items, each with the reason it stopped under its title and its one button: one at approve final review with Review, a checkout that moved with Retry, a running-time cap with Raise cap, a failure at merge request with Retry, an agent's question with Answer, a paused item with Resume, and a spend cap with Raise budget](/assets/stops.png)

## By stop kind
_Added in 2.1_

Every stop has a kind: `stop.kind` on the [HTTP API](/reference/http-api/work-item-object#stopkind) and in the [`work_item_needs_human` event](/reference/events#stop-kinds). If you know the kind, this table sends you to the tables that hold its reasons.

| Kind | What you see | Go to |
|---|---|---|
| `question` | `needs_context: <question>`: an agent asked something the repo could not answer. | [Questions, failed tasks and logins](#questions-failed-tasks-and-logins) |
| `cap` | `<loop> exhausted`, `<gate> exhausted`, `hit its time cap`, `showed no output`, `waited past its timeout` or `rate_limit retries exhausted`. | [Fix loops, gates and reviews](#fix-loops-gates-and-reviews), [Time caps and waits](#time-caps-and-waits) |
| `budget` | `budget cap reached`, `budget_usd reached`, `token budget reached` or `budget_usd cannot be checked`. | [Caps and budgets](#caps-and-budgets) |
| `failed` | `task failed in node <node>`, a changed-test scope that ran red, or a read-only step that changed files. | [Questions, failed tasks and logins](#questions-failed-tasks-and-logins), [Fix loops, gates and reviews](#fix-loops-gates-and-reviews) |
| `conflict` | A merge or rebase conflict the conflict handler could not clear. | [Git state of the worktree](#git-state-of-the-worktree) |
| `mr_closed` | `the merge request was closed on the forge without merging`. | [Fix loops, gates and reviews](#fix-loops-gates-and-reviews) |
| `config` | `could not start <task> ...`, a worktree that cannot be set up, or `this item's one-off chain changed before it started ...`. _Added in next_ | [A task Kraft would not launch](#a-task-kraft-would-not-launch), [Setting up the worktree](#setting-up-the-worktree), [The sandbox and Kits](#the-sandbox-and-kits) |
| `infra` | A crash, a restart that ended the agent, or a forge or git call that failed. | [Restarts and crashes](#restarts-and-crashes), [Questions, failed tasks and logins](#questions-failed-tasks-and-logins) |
| `stuck` | `stuck: ...` or `judge: ...`, or a repair that finished with concerns. | [Fix loops, gates and reviews](#fix-loops-gates-and-reviews), [Escalation turns and things that are not a stop](#escalation-turns-and-things-that-are-not-a-stop) |
| `checkout` | An in-place item could not take its checkout without forcing it, the checkout moved during a task, the agent committed a file you had changed, or your stashed edits clash. _Added in 2.1_ | [Git state of the worktree](#git-state-of-the-worktree), [Clear a checkout stop](/guides/run/run-items-in-place#clear-a-checkout-stop) |

The kinds `gate`, `wait` and `rate_limit` are not here: they are not stops that need you. A pending gate waits for your decision, and the other two are holds that Kraft ends itself. See [Escalation turns and things that are not a stop](#escalation-turns-and-things-that-are-not-a-stop) and [Waiting on CI or a review](#waiting-on-ci-or-a-review).

## Check the cause before you retry

In the web UI, click the item on the board. Its side panel says where it stopped and why, with the one action that fits, and lists what happened last:

![A stopped item's side panel beside the board: Stopped at verification, Running time hit its 8h cap, with Open config, then its status, progress, current node, spend, chain and repo, and its recent events, the newest saying a session capped out](/assets/stop-peek.png)

A retry without the cause reruns the same failure. Read the reason first, with these three commands:

| Command | What it shows |
|---|---|
| `kraft view show ID` | The node it stopped on, and the reason. |
| `kraft view events ID --type work_item_needs_human` | The stop, with every field. |
| `kraft view logs ID` | The log of the session that stopped it. |

Where a fix says **retry**, run `kraft item retry ID`, and add `--steer "..."` to tell the agent what to do differently; a retry you run resets the node's attempt counters. In Claude Code, `/kraft:triage` reads all three and suggests the next step. The [events catalogue](/reference/events#work_item_needs_human) lists every field of a stop.

## Find your reason

The first column is the text Kraft shows, in the item's side panel, in `kraft view show ID` and in the board's stop card. `...` stands for text that varies. A row either holds the whole fix, or links to an entry under [Stop reasons in detail](/troubleshooting/stop-reasons-in-detail).

### Questions, failed tasks and logins

| The reason says | What happened | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| `needs_context: <question>` | The agent asked a question it could not answer from the repo. | Answer it: `kraft item resume ID --steer "<answer>"`. |
| `task failed in node <node>: ...` | An agent task or command failed. | Read `kraft view logs ID`, then retry with a steer that names the fix. |
| `task failed in node <node>: ...`, and the log shows an authentication or login error | The agent CLI is installed but not logged in. | Run the agent once in a terminal and log in (`claude` for the shipped chains), then `kraft item retry ID`. `kraft admin doctor` checks only that it is on `PATH`. |
| A [forge](/concepts/vocabulary#forge) task (opening, syncing or merging the merge request), or a git push or fetch, failed, and the log shows `HTTP 401`, `Bad credentials`, `Authentication failed`, `could not read Username`, `Permission denied (publickey)` or `gh auth login` | Kraft holds no forge token, and the forge refused what `gh`, `glab` or git sent. | See [Forge authentication fails](/troubleshooting/stop-reasons-in-detail#forge-authentication-fails). |
| A forge task failed with `HTTP 429`, or with `HTTP 403` and `rate limit` in the log, and the item shows `rate_limited` instead of a stop. | The forge asked Kraft to wait. Kraft starts the item again when the forge allows it: at the time the forge named, else after a minute. Before, this read as a login failure. _Added in next_ | Nothing. See [A forge rate limit](/troubleshooting/stop-reasons-in-detail#a-forge-rate-limit). |
| The log shows `Could not resolve host`, `Connection timed out` or `Connection refused` on a forge or git call. The stop's `facts.cause` is `forge_unreachable`. | The machine the server runs on could not reach the forge. | Check that machine's network, DNS and proxy settings, then `kraft item retry ID`. |

### A task Kraft would not launch

| The reason says | What happened | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| `could not start <task> in node <node>: ...` | Kraft refused the launch, and the rest of the reason says why. | Fix the named cause, often with `kraft admin doctor`, then retry. The rows below are the common causes. |
| `could not start ...` with `cannot run: backend: auto, but no forge is recorded for this repo` | The repo had no GitHub or GitLab `origin` when you connected it. | See [`no forge is recorded for this repo`](/troubleshooting/stop-reasons-in-detail#no-forge-is-recorded-for-this-repo). |
| `could not start ...` with `gh is not installed or not on PATH`, `glab could not start: ...` or `git cannot run in <folder>: it does not exist` | A forge task's CLI never started on the server's machine: it is missing or cannot run there, or the item's worktree is gone. Before, this read as a failed task. _Added in next_ | Install `gh` or `glab` on that machine (`kraft admin doctor` checks that it is on `PATH`), or put the folder back, then retry. |
| `could not start ...` with `declares neither test_scopes nor test_command in repos.yaml` | The repo has no test command, and Kraft will not guess one. | See [`declares neither test_scopes nor test_command`](/troubleshooting/stop-reasons-in-detail#declares-neither-test_scopes-nor-test_command). |
| `could not start ...` with `stored model override is not a model id: clear it with ...` | A model override on the item or a node is not a model id, such as `sonnet 4`. | See [`stored model override is not a model id`](/troubleshooting/stop-reasons-in-detail#stored-model-override-is-not-a-model-id). |
| `could not start ...` with `add-on <id> <version> could not be read: ...` | The item pinned a version of a [Kraft add-on](/concepts/vocabulary#add-on) whose files are gone from this machine and Kraft could not fetch them again, or whose stored files were edited. The rest says why. | See [`add-on ... could not be read`](/troubleshooting/stop-reasons-in-detail#add-on-could-not-be-read). |
| `could not start ...` with `<repo-id>'s repo library is visible only to items in <repo-id>` | The task's skill, agent profile or fallback is in another repository's [repo library](/reference/configuration/repo-libraries), which only that repository's items read. | Use a skill or profile of your own, an add-on's, or this repository's own library, then retry. See [Who can name it](/reference/configuration/repo-libraries#who-can-name-it). |
| `could not start ...` with `repository '<id>' has no accepted repo library` | The task names `<id>:<name>`, and `<id>` is a connected repository whose library is not accepted. A repository's id is never another tool's plugin. | Accept that library with `kraft repo library`, or name the skill or profile another way. |
| `... nothing registers it for Claude Code: ...` | No `kraft` MCP server is registered, so Kraft refused a Claude worker. | See [`nothing registers it for Claude Code`](/troubleshooting/stop-reasons-in-detail#nothing-registers-it-for-claude-code). |
| `... is not on the PATH the worker gets, so Claude cannot start it: ...` | Claude cannot start the registered `kraft` MCP server, so Kraft refused a Claude worker. _Added in next_ | See [`is not on the PATH the worker gets`](/troubleshooting/stop-reasons-in-detail#is-not-on-the-path-the-worker-gets). |
| `in-place items run unsandboxed; <setting> turns a sandbox on` | The item runs in place, and its repo or a policy turned a sandbox on after it was filed. _Added in 2.1_ | Drop that sandbox, or file the item again with `--worktree`. See [No sandbox, and no Cursor](/guides/run/run-items-in-place#no-sandbox-and-no-cursor). |
| `cursor's permission hook writes .cursor/hooks.json into the folder it runs in; an in-place item cannot use it ...` | The task's harness is Cursor, and its hook would be written into your checkout. _Added in 2.1_ | Pick another harness for the item, then retry. |
| `<checkout>, the checkout this item runs in, is gone: put it back, or abandon the item` | The in-place item's repo folder was moved or deleted. _Added in 2.1_ | Put the folder back where it was, then retry, or abandon the item. |
| `this item's one-off chain changed before it started and needs approval again: ...` | The item's [one-off chain](/guides/day-to-day/file-a-work-item#file-with-a-one-off-chain) changed after Kraft took it to start, so its custom content no longer matches what a person approved. _Added in next_ | Press **Start** on the item, in the web UI or on a phone, then **Approve and start** in the review it opens. Approved through the API, it goes back to `paused` for a person to start. |

### Setting up the worktree

| The reason says | What happened | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| `no user.name configured in <repo>; set it before Kraft can commit` (or `user.email`) | git has no commit identity for the repo, so Kraft would not make the item's [worktree](/concepts/vocabulary#worktree). | See [`no user.name configured`](/troubleshooting/stop-reasons-in-detail#no-username-configured). |
| `no setup_command declared for <repo> in repos.yaml, so <id>'s worktree cannot be prepared. Declare one ...` | The repo has no `setup_command`, and Kraft will not guess what to run in a fresh worktree. | See [`no setup_command declared`](/troubleshooting/stop-reasons-in-detail#no-setup_command-declared). |
| `setup command failed for <id>: '<command>': <output>` | The repo's `setup_command` ran in the new worktree and exited non-zero, and the reason ends with its output. | In the worktree (`cd "$(kraft repo path ID)"`), run the command yourself. Fix it, or the repo's `setup_command`, then `kraft item retry ID`. |

### Fix loops, gates and reviews

| The reason says | What happened | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| `<loop> exhausted after N fix cycle(s)` | A [fix loop](/concepts/vocabulary#fix-loop) spent its attempts without passing. | Read the findings (`kraft view events ID --type findings_measured`), then retry with a steer. |
| `<node>.on_base_changed exhausted after N restart(s) from '<node>'` | The [base branch](/concepts/vocabulary#base-branch) kept moving: each restart from the named node found it moved again, until the restarts ran out. | Retry once the base branch settles; a retry resets the count. To allow more restarts, raise that key under `loops:` in `policy.yaml`. The shipped chain restarts `merge_request_feedback` from `verification`. |
| `dropped from the merge train in node <node>: <reason>; ...; Kraft never re-queues a merge request on its own` (or `merge queue`) | Someone took the merge request off the [merge train or queue](/reference/chain-nodes#merge-trains-and-merge-queues), the forge gave a reason Kraft cannot read, or the node declares no [`on_merge_rejected`](/reference/chain-nodes#on_merge_rejected). Kraft never re-queues it and sends no escalation agent. _Added in 2.3_ | If you removed it on purpose, decide what comes next. Otherwise queue it again on the forge, or `kraft item retry ID`. |
| `resume: the merge-rejection repair in node <node> did not finish: <reason>` | Kraft restarted mid-repair of a merge train or queue rejection, or mid-restart after one. If a repair had started or the branch moved, the item is held at the restart node with its gates reopened. _Added in 2.3_ | Read the repair's log (`kraft view logs ID`), then `kraft item retry ID`. On the merge node the retry finishes the restart or repairs again, never queueing the rejected head. |
| `the merge-rejection repair in node <node> committed nothing: <reason>` (or `committed nothing in <checkout>` for a workspace member) | The train or queue dropped the merge request and the repair changed nothing there, so queueing it again would only be rejected again. A commit in another checkout holds the item at the restart node. _Added in 2.3_ | Read the repair's log (`kraft view logs ID`), then `kraft item retry ID --steer "..."` naming the fix: the repair runs again with it, and its commit goes through `final_review`. |
| `the merge-rejection repair in node <node> could not fix it: <reason>`, or `needs_context: <question>` after a drop | The repair failed, or asked a question the repo could not answer, such as when the fix belongs in a repository that already merged. _Added in 2.3_ | Answer it, then `kraft item retry ID --steer "..."`: the repair runs again with your answer. If it had committed, the item is held at the restart node and the retry goes through `final_review` again. |
| `<node>.on_merge_rejected exhausted after N restart(s) from '<node>'` | The merge train or queue kept rejecting the merge request: each repair and restart was rejected again, until the rewinds ran out. The stop kind is `cap`. _Added in 2.3_ | Read the drops (`kraft view events ID --type merge_queue_dropped`) for what keeps failing, fix it, then retry; a retry resets the count. To allow more rewinds, raise that key under `loops:` in `policy.yaml`. |
| `<gate> exhausted after N rejection(s)` | A gate was rejected as many times as its chain allows. | Retry with a steer, or rework the spec or plan yourself. |
| `stuck: ...` or `judge: ...` | A fix loop made no progress across cycles (`stuck`), or the loop's [judge](/concepts/vocabulary#judge) decided another repair attempt is not justified (`judge`). | Kraft has already tried an [escalation turn](/concepts/vocabulary#escalation-turn) on its own. Send a message with `kraft item escalate ID --message "..."`, or retry with a steer. |
| `the merge request was closed on the forge without merging` | Someone or something other than Kraft closed the item's merge request without merging it. | The board offers **Reopen !N**, **Open a new MR** (only when the item had opened one) and **Cancel item…**. See [MR closed externally](/reference/statuses#mr-closed-externally) (the stop's `stop.kind` is `mr_closed`). |
| `a repair in node <node> finished with concerns ...` | A repair ran, but its agent doubted the result. | Read the concerns in the reason, then retry with a steer, or `kraft item skip ID`. |
| `<scope> is read_only, but it changed the worktree: ...` | A read-only step edited files. | Look at the named files, then retry. |

### Git state of the worktree

| The reason says | What happened | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| `the conflict in node <node> cannot be resolved ...` | Rebasing onto the base branch hit a conflict nobody resolved. | Resolve it in the worktree (`cd "$(kraft repo path ID)"`), commit, then retry. |
| `untracked <paths> would be overwritten by the base branch; move or delete it, then retry`, or `git would not start the rebase onto the base branch ...` | Kraft went to rebase the item onto its base branch, and git refused to start, so nothing was rebased. | See [Git would not start the rebase](/troubleshooting/stop-reasons-in-detail#git-would-not-start-the-rebase). |
| `<worktree> has a <name> in progress: inspect it, then delete ...` | The worktree holds a rebase, merge, cherry-pick, revert, bisect or autostash, so Kraft would not rebase or commit over it. | See [A git operation is in progress](/troubleshooting/stop-reasons-in-detail#a-git-operation-is-in-progress). |
| `<worktree> is not on <branch>; check it out by hand, then retry` | The worktree's HEAD names another branch, so Kraft would not commit, rebase or push from it. | `cd "$(kraft repo path ID)"`, check that nothing of yours is on the named branch, `git checkout <branch>`, then retry. |
| `origin/<branch> is at <sha>, which Kraft did not push and this worktree does not have ...` | Someone pushed commits to the item's merge request branch, and Kraft's push would have overwritten them. Kraft's own earlier pushes no longer stop it here, even one it stopped before recording. | `cd "$(kraft repo path ID)"`, `git pull --rebase origin <branch>` to bring them in, then retry. |
| `git would not switch <checkout> from <branch> to <item branch>: it would overwrite <files>` | An in-place item went to put your checkout on its branch, and git refused because the switch would overwrite your uncommitted changes to those files. Nothing was changed. _Added in 2.1_ | Commit, stash or move those changes, then retry. |
| `switching <checkout> from <branch> to <item branch> would overwrite <files>, ignored here but tracked on <item branch>` | Your ignored files sit where the item's branch tracks files, and git would replace them without asking. _Added in 2.1_ | Move them aside, then retry. |
| `no user.email configured in <checkout> ...` (or `user.name`) | The in-place item could not commit its spec or plan, because your checkout has no git identity. _Added in 2.1_ | Set it with `git config`, then retry. |
| `<path> in <checkout> is not the <spec or plan> this item was filed with ...` | A file of yours sits where the in-place item's attachment goes, so Kraft did not overwrite it. _Added in 2.1_ | Move the file aside, then retry. |
| `<checkout> has unmerged paths: ...` or `<checkout> has a git operation in progress ...` | Your checkout is mid-merge, mid-rebase or holds conflicted files, so the in-place item did not switch or rebase it, or a task ended with it that way (`at the end of the run of <task>`). _Added in 2.1_ | Finish or abort it yourself, then retry. |
| `the branch <branch> is gone from <checkout> ...` | The in-place item's branch was deleted, and Kraft does not make it again. _Added in 2.1_ | Restore it from `git reflog` and retry, or abandon the item. |
| `the checkout moved to <branch> during the run of <task> in <checkout> ...` | Someone switched your checkout off the in-place item's branch while its task ran. Kraft did not switch it back, nor move any commit. _Added in 2.1_ | Check the commits the reason lists, then retry once the checkout may go back. See [After the checkout moved](/guides/run/run-items-in-place#after-the-checkout-moved). |
| `<task> left commits made or moved onto during it that are not on <item branch> ...` | The checkout left the item's branch and came back during the task, so its work may be elsewhere. _Added in 2.1_ | Check where those commits belong, then retry: the task runs again. |
| `the agent committed <files>, which already had uncommitted changes before it started ...` | The in-place agent committed files you had already changed, so your edits may be in its commit. Nothing was undone. _Added in 2.1_ | Read the commit, move anything of yours out of it, then retry. |
| `your edits to <files> clash with the newer <base>; your original edits are safe in the stash as <sha> ...`, or `... clash with what <task> rebased onto ...` | Rebasing the in-place item, by Kraft or by the agent resolving a conflict, put your stashed edits back with conflict markers. _Added in 2.1_ | See [Recover your stashed edits](/guides/run/run-items-in-place#recover-your-stashed-edits). |
| `Kraft's rebase of <checkout> onto the newer <base> could not put your edits back ...`, or `the rebase <task> ran in <checkout> could not put your edits back ...` | Something wrote your stashed files while the in-place item rebased, so git refused to put your edits back. They are in the stash, not the checkout. _Added in 2.1_ | Run `git stash apply <sha>` with the sha the reason names, then retry. |
| `rebasing <checkout> onto the newer <base> would overwrite <files>, ignored here but tracked on <base>` | The rebase would replace files your checkout ignores. Nothing was rebased. _Added in 2.1_ | Move them aside, then retry. |
| `another item, <title>, is using the checkout <checkout> ...` | Another in-place item took the checkout between this item's turn in the queue and its switch, so Kraft did not take it from under that item. _Added in 2.1_ | Retry once that item stops. |
| `<checkout> left <branch> before Kraft could rebase it ...` | Someone switched your checkout off the in-place item's branch just before its rebase, so nothing was rebased. _Added in 2.1_ | Retry the item. |
| `<checkout> is on a detached HEAD with commits no branch holds (<shas>) ...` | Switching to the in-place item's branch would leave those commits reachable only from the reflog, so Kraft did not switch. _Added in 2.1_ | Put a branch on them with `git branch <name>`, then retry. |
| `<checkout> left <branch> right after Kraft switched to it ...` | A hook or a person moved your checkout off the in-place item's branch before Kraft committed its spec or plan, so nothing was committed. _Added in 2.1_ | Retry the item. |
| `a rebase Kraft did not start is under way in <checkout> ...` | Your checkout is in the middle of a rebase of yours, so Kraft did not rebase the in-place item there. _Added in 2.1_ | Finish or abort it yourself, then retry. |
| `Kraft could not read <checkout> ...`, or `Kraft could not check <branch> out apart from <checkout> to rebase it ...` | A git command the in-place item needs failed, and the reason ends with what git said. _Added in 2.1_ | Fix that, then retry. |
| `the branch adds Git LFS files and this machine has no git-lfs ...` | The item's new commits hold Git LFS pointers, and Kraft uploads their objects itself before it pushes, with git-lfs. Nothing was pushed. _Added in next_ | Install git-lfs on the machine Kraft runs on, then retry. |
| `origin no longer has <branch>, which Kraft pushed ...` | The item's branch was deleted from origin, usually with its merge request merged or closed, and Kraft will not recreate it. | To publish the work anyway, run the two `git update-ref -d` commands the reason names in the item's worktree, then retry. Otherwise abandon the item. |

### Caps and budgets

| The reason says | What happened | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| `budget cap reached: ... on this work item` | The item's own dollar cap refused the next launch. | Raise that cap and retry in one step: `kraft item raise-budget ID --usd N`, or **Raise cap** on the item. See [Raise a cap on a stopped item](/guides/day-to-day/raise-a-cap). |
| `budget_usd reached ...`, `token budget reached ...` | A policy `budget_usd` or `token_budget` refused the next launch. | See [A policy budget or token cap refused the launch](/troubleshooting/stop-reasons-in-detail#a-policy-budget-or-token-cap-refused-the-launch). |
| `budget cap reached: ... today, across every work item` | The daily cap, which nothing on the item raises, refused the next launch. | Raise `budget.daily_usd` in `policy.yaml`, or wait for local midnight, then retry. `raise-budget` is refused, and the item's page offers **Retry** with no raise. |
| `budget_usd cannot be checked: N launch(es) in ... reported no cost, and unknown spend is never counted as free ...` | A launch ran on a [harness](/concepts/vocabulary#harness) that reports no cost, on a model Kraft cannot price, so it cannot show the scope is under its `budget_usd`. | See [A budget cap cannot be checked](/troubleshooting/stop-reasons-in-detail#a-budget-cap-cannot-be-checked). |

### Time caps and waits

| The reason says | What happened | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| `<scope> hit its time cap of N minutes` | A time cap ran out. | Raise it for this item, then retry: `kraft item set-policy ID --policy time_cap_minutes=240`. See [Raise a cap on a stopped item](/guides/day-to-day/raise-a-cap). |
| `<task> showed no output for N minutes` | An agent session wrote nothing to its log for `agent_idle_minutes`. | Read its log (`kraft view logs ID`), then retry. If it was working, raise `agent_idle_minutes` in `policy.yaml`. _Added in 2.3_ |
| `<scope> hit its total time cap of N minutes` | A scope's wall-clock cap, which waits and gates count toward, ran out. | Raise it for this item, then retry: `kraft item set-policy ID --policy total_time_cap_minutes=N`. A node or task needs its path in the key, such as `merge_request_feedback.ci.await_ci.total_time_cap_minutes=N`. |
| `gate <gate> waited past its timeout of N minutes` | Nobody decided a gate in time. | Retry to reopen the gate, then decide it. |
| `<task> timed out waiting ...` | An external wait (CI, a review) ran out. | See [Waiting on CI or a review](#waiting-on-ci-or-a-review). Retry once the outside is fixed. |
| `rate_limit retries exhausted after N attempt(s)` | The agent's provider kept refusing for a rate limit. | Wait for the limit to reset, then retry. |
| A CI or automated-review failure, with a suggested **retry** | CI failed for its own reasons, or a cancelled run has no successor. | Retry once CI has recovered. |

### The sandbox and Kits

These stops have their own page, [Sandbox and Kit stops](/troubleshooting/sandbox-and-kit-stops). Each row below links to the entry for its text.

| The reason says | See |
|---|---|

| The reason says | What happened | Fix |

## Escalation turns and things that are not a stop

Some stops reach you only after Kraft has tried an **escalation turn** on its
own, up to three times (`auto_escalate_stuck_cap` in `policy.yaml`): a task
that failed after its recovery, an exhausted fix loop, and a `stuck:` stop.
`kraft view logs ID` shows those turns. The other stops (a configuration or
forge problem, a budget or time cap, a wait that ran out) come to you at once.

Three things look like a stop and are not one, or need a different move:

- A pending gate is not a stop. It waits for your decision:
  `kraft item approve ID` or `kraft item reject ID --note "..."`.
- If the work is heading the wrong way rather than failing, pause it and
  resume it with a steer: `kraft item pause ID`, then
  `kraft item resume ID --steer "..."`.
- To move past a node without running it, use `kraft item skip ID`.

## Waiting on CI or a review

Kraft keeps checking on both waits. One that outlasts its timeout stops the item with `<task> timed out waiting`. [A step waiting for a machine](#waiting-for-a-machine) never stops the item with `timed out waiting`.

### `merge_request_feedback` waits on "no checks yet"

The pull request has no CI checks, so nothing will ever pass. Do one of these:

- Add CI that runs on pull requests.
- Set `ci_checks: false` on the repo's entry in [`repos.yaml`](/reference/configuration/repos), so both CI waits, before and after the merge, pass at once.
- File the item on the `quick-task` chain.

A pull request that conflicts with its base branch does not wait here, although GitHub runs no checks on it. Kraft rebases the branch onto the base at once, and a conflict the rebase cannot resolve stops the item instead of the wait running out.

### Waiting for a machine

_Added in 2.4_

A step placed on a machine waits while that machine is offline, full or disabled. A step placed on a group, or on `any`, waits while none of its machines is free. The item shows `waiting for gpu-box (full)`, `waiting for gpu-box (offline: ...)`, `waiting for gpu-box (disabled)`, `waiting for a machine in gpu` or `waiting for any machine allowed for kraft`.

Kraft never runs the step anywhere else, and the wait does not count against the step's `time_cap_minutes`; a `total_time_cap_minutes` still counts it. Run `kraft admin worker list`: an offline machine shows why, and a full one shows its slots in use.

`waiting for gpu-box (disabled)` means someone turned the machine's `enabled` off, so it takes no new steps. It wins over `offline` in the text, because it is a choice. `kraft admin worker enable gpu-box` ends the wait.

`waiting for local (2 of 2 running)` is a local step waiting for a slot of the local worker, which `local: capacity` in [`workers.yaml`](/reference/configuration/workers#the-local-worker) limits. It has no session yet. `waiting for local (disabled)` is the same wait with `local: enabled: false`.

The step starts when the machine is back or a slot frees; one on a group starts on the first member that frees, and Kraft looks again every 30 seconds. To run it elsewhere, change the placement with `kraft item set-node-override --node N --runner local`.

### On GitLab, which pipeline counts

_Added in 2.2_

Kraft reads the newest pipeline that ran for the branch's head commit, branch pipelines and merge request pipelines alike. A merged results pipeline counts too. It runs on a merge commit of the branch into its target, and Kraft accepts it when that merge commit was built from the current head.

Right after a push the log reads `no pipeline for <sha> yet` until GitLab starts a pipeline for the new head. A line after it naming a merged results pipeline says why that one was not taken: it tested another commit, or its merge commit could not be read.

### `external_approval` keeps waiting

A branch rule on the forge requires an approving review. Have someone else approve the pull request, or remove the rule. Kraft checks again on its own.
